# How to Submit a Proof to Clay Mathematics Institute

## ⚠️ **CRITICAL RULE: Direct Submission is NOT Allowed**

**You CANNOT send your proof directly to Clay Mathematics Institute.**

All proofs must be published through official channels first. CMI explicitly states:
> "CMI will not accept Proposed Solutions submitted directly to CMI and is under no obligation to provide explanation or justification."

---

## 📋 **OFFICIAL SUBMISSION PROCESS (3 Stages)**

### **STAGE 1: PUBLISH IN A QUALIFYING JOURNAL** (Year 0)

#### Step 1A: Choose a Peer-Reviewed Journal

Your proof MUST be published in a journal meeting **ALL** these criteria:

**Required Characteristics:**
- ✅ Has a **named editorial board** with public contact information
- ✅ Editor/board members can identify qualified peer reviewers
- ✅ Has a **formal peer-review process**
- ✅ Listed in **MathSciNet** (maintained by American Mathematical Society)
- ✅ Peer reviewers confirm expert review in your Problem's field

**What does NOT qualify:**
- ❌ ArXiv (preprints only)
- ❌ Self-published papers
- ❌ Blogs, wikis, or personal websites
- ❌ Journals without editorial boards
- ❌ Journals not in MathSciNet
- ❌ Non-peer-reviewed venues

**CMI Position:**
- Does NOT recommend specific journals
- Does NOT maintain a pre-approved journal list
- Does NOT certify whether YOUR chosen journal qualifies
- Makes sole final decisions about acceptability

#### Step 1B: Submit Your Proof to the Journal

- Write your proof in standard mathematical format
- Follow the journal's submission guidelines
- Submit through the journal's official channel
- **Do NOT contact CMI at this stage**

#### Step 1C: Complete Peer Review

- Work with journal editors on revisions
- Address reviewer feedback
- Achieve acceptance and publication

**Timeline:** 6 months – 2+ years (depending on journal)

---

### **STAGE 2: WAIT 2 YEARS FOR COMMUNITY ACCEPTANCE** (Years 1-2)

#### Step 2A: Publish Your Paper

Once published, the 2-year clock starts.

#### Step 2B: Achieve "General Acceptance in the Global Mathematics Community"

During these 2 years, CMI monitors whether your solution has earned widespread acceptance by:

**Indicators of Acceptance:**
- ✅ Independent mathematicians cite and discuss your work
- ✅ Published papers reference your solution
- ✅ International conferences feature your work
- ✅ Books include your results
- ✅ Other researchers build on your findings
- ✅ General consensus emerges in your field
- ✅ Awards or recognition from mathematical societies

**CMI's Criteria:**
> "A plausible potential solution to any of these hard problems will be 'effectively unmissable to all but the most superficial monitoring' of the mathematical and related literature."

**You do NOT need to:**
- Get explicit endorsements from famous mathematicians
- Submit letters of support
- Invite CMI to review your work
- Contact CMI at all during this period

**CMI will:**
- Monitor the mathematics community independently
- Track citations and discussions
- Assess consensus organically
- Make NO public announcements about your work's status

**Timeline:** Exactly 2 years minimum

---

### **STAGE 3: CMI FORMAL EVALUATION** (After 2+ Years)

#### Step 3A: CMI Decides to Proceed (or Not)

After 2+ years and evidence of community acceptance, CMI will:

- **Option A:** Decide detailed consideration is merited → proceed to advisory review
- **Option B:** Decide consideration is not merited → no Prize awarded, no explanation given

**Important:** CMI has complete discretion and will NOT explain their decision.

#### Step 3B: CMI Convenes Special Advisory Committee

If CMI proceeds, they establish a committee with:
- **Minimum 3 members**
- **At least 2 field experts** (selected by CMI)
- **Independent external reviewers**
- Experts typically NOT from your institution

#### Step 3C: Committee Reviews Your Work

- Conducts thorough, detailed examination
- May consult additional experts
- Reports findings to CMI
- Process takes "reasonable time" (undefined by CMI)

#### Step 3D: CMI Makes Final Decision

CMI determines:
- ✅ Whether to award the Prize
- 💰 Amount (may split if multiple contributors)
- 🤝 Whether to recognize prior work (co-awards possible)
- 🏆 Consolation prizes for counterexamples

## 🎯 **SPECIFIC GUIDELINES BY PROBLEM**

### **P vs NP**
- Can prove P = NP or P ≠ NP
- Either direction qualifies for full Prize
- Must completely resolve the question
- Publication in top CS/Math journal recommended (e.g., J. ACM, SIAM, Annals of Math)

### **Riemann Hypothesis**
- Must prove all non-trivial zeros lie on Re(s) = 1/2
- OR prove the equivalent functional equation formulation
- Publication in math/number theory journal
- Recommended: Annals of Mathematics, Inventiones Mathematicae, Acta Mathematica

### **Yang-Mills & Mass Gap**
- Must establish rigorous existence of Yang-Mills theory on ℝ⁴
- Must prove mass gap > 0 for any compact simple gauge group
- Publication in physics/math journal
- Recommended: Communications in Mathematical Physics, Journal of High Energy Physics

### **Navier-Stokes**
- Must prove or disprove: smooth solutions exist globally in time for finite-energy initial data
- Resolution in either direction qualifies
- Publication in applied math/PDE journal
- Recommended: Annals of Mathematics, Journal of the American Mathematical Society

## 💡 **KEY SUCCESS FACTORS**

1. **Rigor First:** Your proof must withstand 2+ years of scrutiny from world experts
2. **Right Journal:** Publication venue signals quality to CMI and mathematical community
3. **Peer Validation:** Independent mathematicians must accept and build on your work
4. **Patience:** The 2-year period exists specifically to ensure solution is correct
5. **Community Engagement:** Present at conferences, respond to feedback, clarify results
6. **Completeness:** Answer all questions in the original Problem description
